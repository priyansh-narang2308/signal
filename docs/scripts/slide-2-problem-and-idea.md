# Slide 2: Problem and idea

**Time:** about 55 seconds
**On screen:** the problem quote, Dashboards / Summarisers / SIGNAL on the left, the three expectations on the right

---

Start with the problem. A non-obvious finding is, almost by definition, **not written down anywhere.** If it were in an annual report or a news article, it wouldn't be non-obvious. So a system that retrieves documents can only give back what someone has already said.

_[Point to the left column.]_

Today's tools stop there. **Dashboards** show the headline ratios, but not what's driving them. **Summarisers** repeat management's story. Even when you ask them to criticise it, they argue; they don't test.

_[Point to the right column.]_

SIGNAL takes a different approach. For any company, it builds **three independent expectations.**

- **Self:** what the company's own history predicts, broken down into its drivers.
- **Peer:** what similar companies did in the same quarter.
- **Narrative:** what management said was happening.

When the numbers **keep breaking all three**, we have a candidate. Not a discovery yet, just something worth investigating.
