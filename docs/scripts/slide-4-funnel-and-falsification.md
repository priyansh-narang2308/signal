# Slide 4: Funnel and falsification

**Time:** about 50 seconds
**On screen:** the funnel from 143 to 1 on the left, the nine tests on the right

---

Here is what that filtering looks like in practice.

*[Point to the funnel.]*

In our example run, SIGNAL started with **143 candidate breaches.** 31 were structurally valid. 11 had independent corroboration. 4 were specific to the company. 2 survived falsification. **One became the discovery.**

Nothing here is hand-picked, and **every rejection is logged with its reason.** That matters, because it lets you check what the system threw away, not only what it kept.

*[Point to the list of tests.]*

On the right is how we try to break each finding: **nine alternative explanations, registered before we see any results.** Is it a sector-wide effect? A regulatory change? An accounting change or a merger? Just a ratio being diluted by fast loan growth? A one-off quarter? Something management already disclosed?

Each one is a test **written in code that runs on the data**, not a prompt. The strongest explanation that survives becomes the counterargument in the final report.
